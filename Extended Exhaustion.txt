# Extended Exhaustion

Exhaustion is measured in levels from 0 to 30. A creature dies if its exhaustion level reaches 31.


## Exhaustion Effects

Each level of exhaustion imposes cumulative penalties as shown on the **Extended Exhaustion** table.

Choose one of the following methods for applying exhaustion penalties.

#### Method A: Sequential Progression

When a creature gains exhaustion, fill the table from left to right, completing each row before moving to the next.

With this method, exhaustion levels 1–6 introduce each penalty type. Levels 7–12 increase each penalty by 1, levels 13–18 increase each penalty by 2, and so on.

#### Method B: Player Choice

When you gain exhaustion, choose which column to mark. You can distribute your exhaustion levels across the six penalty types as you choose.

This allows you to customize how exhaustion affects your character based on your priorities and playstyle.

## Gaining Exhaustion

A creature gains 1 level of exhaustion when it:

* Drops to 0 hit points.
* Travels for more than 8 hours in a day.
* Goes without food or water, as described in the existing rules in chapter 8 of the *Player's Handbook*.
* Suffers another effect that would normally cause exhaustion.
* Endures other harsh conditions at the DM's discretion, such as extreme weather, magical curses, or similar hazards.

## Removing Exhaustion

## Long Rest Recovery

At the end of a long rest, you remove 1 level of exhaustion.

Then, make a Constitution saving throw with a DC equal to **30 minus your current exhaustion level**.

On a success, you remove 1 additional level of exhaustion and repeat this saving throw.

Continue making saving throws in this way until you fail a saving throw or your exhaustion level reaches 0.

### Example

A creature with 12 levels of exhaustion finishes a long rest.

It first removes 1 level of exhaustion, reducing its exhaustion level to 11. It then makes a Constitution saving throw against a DC of 19 (30 − 11).

If it succeeds, it removes another level of exhaustion, reducing its exhaustion level to 10, and makes another Constitution saving throw, this time against a DC of 20 (30 − 10).

The creature continues making saving throws until it fails one or reaches 0 exhaustion.

## Other Recovery

Spells and other magical effects that remove exhaustion function normally, removing the number of levels specified by the effect.

## Special Rules

### Minimum Values

Exhaustion cannot reduce an ability check or saving throw below 1.

### Speed Reduction

If exhaustion reduces your speed to 0 feet or less, your speed becomes 1 foot instead.

### Hit Point Maximum

When exhaustion reduces your hit point maximum, your current hit points are reduced accordingly if they exceed your new maximum.

If this reduction would reduce you to 0 hit points, you instead drop to 1 hit point.

### Unconscious Creatures

Except for the Death Save Reroll, unconscious creature cannot voluntarily spend exhaustion.

## Spending Exhaustion

Exhaustion represents a creature pushing itself beyond its normal limits. A creature can voluntarily gain exhaustion to fuel desperate actions or magical power.

Unless otherwise stated, a creature can use an option below whenever it meets that option's requirements.



### 1st-Level Spell Slot

***1 Exhaustion.***

If you have no spell slots remaining, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to cast a 1st-level spell.

### Upcast

***1+ Exhaustion.***

You can gain 1 level of exhaustion per spell level to cast a spell at a higher level.

If you cast a spell beyond the highest level of spell slot normally available to you, the cost is 2 levels of exhaustion per spell level.

### Extra Attack

***1 Exhaustion.***

When you take the Attack action, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to make one additional weapon attack as part of that action.

### Rage

***2 Exhaustion.***

If you have no uses of Rage remaining, you can gain 2 levels of exhaustion to enter your Rage.

### Reckless Attack Enhancement

***1 Exhaustion.***

When you make melee weapon attacks during your turn, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to gain advantage on all of those attack rolls, rather than only the first attack roll made using Reckless Attack.

### Brutal Critical

***1 Exhaustion.***

When you score a critical hit, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to roll one additional weapon damage die as part of the critical hit.

### Ki Points

***1 Exhaustion.***

If you have no ki points remaining, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to recover 2 ki points.

If you shout **"Kaioken!"** when using this feature, you instead recover 3 ki points.

### Negate Critical Hit

***1 Exhaustion; Reaction.***

When you would take a critical hit, you can use your reaction and gain 1 level of exhaustion to turn the critical hit into a normal hit.

### Extra Reaction

***1 Exhaustion.***

You can gain 1 level of exhaustion to take an additional reaction before the start of your next turn.

You can take only one reaction per trigger.

### Crucial Save

***1 Exhaustion.***

When you make a saving throw, you can gain 1 level of exhaustion to add a +5 bonus to the roll.

You can use this option after rolling the d20, but before the DM determines whether the roll succeeds or fails.

### Perfect Timing

***1 Exhaustion.***

You can gain 1 level of exhaustion to act immediately, regardless of your position in the initiative order.

This does not grant you an additional turn. Instead, you move to the top of the initiative order and act normally on this turn and all subsequent turns.

### Reroll Death Save

***2 Exhaustion; Reaction.***

When you fail a death saving throw, you can use your reaction and gain 2 levels of exhaustion to reroll the saving throw.

You must use the new result.

### Catch Your Breath

***1 Exhaustion.***

You can gain 1 level of exhaustion to regain hit points equal to one seventh of your hit point maximum, to a minimum of 1.

You cannot use this option at 0 hit points.
